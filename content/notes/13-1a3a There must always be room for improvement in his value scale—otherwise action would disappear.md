[[David Deutsch |Deutsch]] would likely argue that to the extent that there is objective reality out there, with regards to the understanding of that reality there is objective improvement. But in *The Evolution of Culture*, he also emphasized how [[1-1c2a There is no escape we are and will be shaped by anti-rational memes |we cannot do away with anti-rational memes]]. [[Daniel Everett |Everett]] would likely agree with Deutsch here—although science deals with the objective reality, scientific theory is fallible and cannot comprehend all of human knowledge (i.e., *the dark matter* part of the mind). Put differently, **the judgement of whether one is objectively improving or not can only make sense in the domains where there is something objectively real—in other domains, you cannot define what “*improvement* in his value scale” means, because *that* is also subjective**. And such subjective preferences can be explained *objectively* only by the all-knowing—but we’ve already deduced that [[13-1a0 The multiverse implies both explanation and action |all-knowing wouldn’t act]]. The subjective value theory, another axiom of praxeology, is retained even with the existence of objective reality and associated scientific theories, precisely because of the multiverse—the subjective preferences won’t go away because [[1-2 We are fallible |we are fallible]].

“Since man is always acting, he must always be engaged in trying to attain the *greatest height on his value scale*. **There must *always* be room for improvement in his value scale; otherwise action would disappear**. This means that there is always open to each actor the prospect of improving his lot, of attaining a value higher than he is giving up, i.e., of *making a psychic profit*.”
